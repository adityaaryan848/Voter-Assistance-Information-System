Indian Election History & Milestones

A look back at the democratic journey of the world's largest democracy.

The Roots of Democracy: Ancient Vaishali

Long before modern constitutions were written, the seeds of democracy were sown in Vaishali (in present-day Bihar, India) around 600 BCE. The Licchavi Republic of Vaishali is widely considered one of the world's first republics (Ganatantra). They had a sophisticated system of governance where representatives were elected by an assembly of citizens, proving that democratic ideals are deeply rooted in ancient Indian civilization.

1950 - Election Commission Established

The Election Commission of India (ECI) was established on 25th January 1950, a day now celebrated as National Voters' Day.

1951-1952 - First General Elections

India held its first massive democratic elections over 4 months, with universal adult suffrage, defying global skeptics.

1982 - Introduction of EVMs

Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) were first used on an experimental basis in the Parur Assembly Constituency in Kerala.

1989 - Voting Age Lowered

The 61st Constitutional Amendment lowered the voting age from 21 years to 18 years, enfranchising millions of youth.

1993 - Introduction of EPIC

Electors Photo Identity Cards (EPIC), commonly known as Voter ID cards, were introduced to prevent electoral fraud.

2013 - Implementation of NOTA

The Supreme Court directed the ECI to introduce the "None of the Above" (NOTA) option on EVMs.

2014 - VVPAT Introduction

Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) was introduced in general elections to ensure transparency and verify votes cast.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common queries about the Indian electoral process.

Who is eligible to vote in India?

Any Indian citizen who is 18 years of age or older on the qualifying date (typically Jan 1st of the year) and is a resident of the polling area, provided they are not otherwise disqualified.

Can Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) vote?

Yes, NRIs holding a valid Indian passport who have not acquired citizenship of another country can vote. They must fill Form 6A to register and must cast their vote in person at their respective constituency.

What happens if I lose my Voter ID (EPIC)?

You can still vote if your name is on the electoral roll by showing an alternative ECI-approved photo ID (like Aadhaar, PAN, Passport). To get a replacement EPIC, you can fill Form 8 for a duplicate card.

Is it mandatory to vote in India?

No, voting is a democratic right, but it is not legally compulsory in India.

How are disputes regarding elections resolved?

Election disputes are strictly handled through an "Election Petition" filed before the respective State High Court. The Election Commission does not have the power to adjudicate post-election disputes.
